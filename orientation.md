#### Begin Orientation:
Minecart Speed Features (MSF) is a fairly small, (currently) mixin-only Fabric mod targeting Minecraft 1.21.1 and Java 21. The current version is `0.0.32`. Versioning lives in `gradle.properties`.

The central movement invariant is to preserve vanilla `moveOnRail`, not replace it. `MinecartRailTraversalMixin.java` redirects the tick's single rail call into a rail-by-rail loop, invoking the real vanilla method once for every rail visited. Vanilla and other mods still own the behavior inside each `moveOnRail` call.

`RailTraversalContext.java` coordinates each segment. `RailMovementBudget.java` owns the single unit of simulation time available to one tick. Each `moveOnRail` call proposes full-tick movement; the budget scales that movement by the remaining tick fraction, clips it at the current rail boundary, and consumes time according to the forward displacement `Entity.move` actually achieved. Crossing several rails must never grant several ticks of movement. If vanilla collision resolution shortens the requested movement, the segment is considered blocked and traversal stops rather than treating the collision point as a rail boundary. `RailResolver.java` and `RailGeometry.java` handle rail ownership, endpoints, and transitions. Activator rails are processed once per visited rail. The 4096-rail traversal limit is a corruption/loop guard, not a speed limit.

`EntityMovementMixin.java` is a narrowly scoped collision/freezing fix. Vanilla `Entity.move` normally discards movement below a small `1.0E-7` cutoff. For minecarts only, this mixin changes that cutoff to zero so any nonzero displacement accepted by vanilla collision resolution is committed. Its purpose is to prevent tiny valid minecart movements from being discarded at contact boundaries which resulted in a minecart freeze scenario where velocity was !=0, but movement would be clamped to 0.

Minecart speed is separate from traversal. `GameRulesMixin.java` registers `minecartMaxSpeed`, which defaults to 8 blocks/second and accepts 1–1000. `MinecartSpeedMixin.java` applies that limit through vanilla `getMaxSpeed`, replaces vanilla's separate 2-block/tick internal rail cap so higher configured speeds are reachable, and retains the water speed reduction. We don't currently correct for the 0.75 maximum velocity multiplier applied when a cart is occupied. This is intended for the time being.

Ascending-rail launch behavior is also separate. `MinecartLaunchMixin.java` listens to traversal boundaries through `RailTraversalListener` and applies the reverse-engineered "Minecart Backport" mod version 1.1.0-style launch when a cart leaves the upper end of an ascending rail. The pure calculations live in `LaunchPhysics.java`.

Living-entity collisions are handled by `MinecartCollisionMixin.java`. Minecarts are explicitly collidable, just like boats, but vanilla still owns physical contact placement. Around the `Entity.move` call inside `moveOnRail`, the mixin captures the incoming cart velocity, lets vanilla resolve movement to the collision point, then handles a qualifying living-entity impact on the server. The traversal layer sees the shortened movement as blocked and ends that rail segment normally.

`ImpactPhysics.java` owns the collision math. Speeds at or below 2 blocks/second have no damage potential. Above that threshold, speed maps through a piecewise-linear damage-potential curve whose final slope continues through the mod's current 1000 blocks/second maximum. On impact, the target's maximum health is subtracted from that potential and the remainder is converted back into residual cart speed. Current health, armor, shields, absorption, entity type, and the damage gamerule do not affect this speed calculation.

`minecartDamagePercent` controls the damage actually dealt, independently of the residual-speed calculation. It defaults to 100 and accepts 1–200. `MinecartCollisionMixin` multiplies the calculated damage potential by this percentage before applying damage. Changing this gamerule does not change how much speed an entity consumes.

Impact knockback is explicit rather than vanilla damage knockback. Horizontal knockback uses 80% of the cart's incoming horizontal velocity, while vertical knockback uses 20% of its total incoming speed. `MinecartImpactDamageSource.java` defines the `minecart_impact` damage source. The minecart is the direct source, while the first passenger, when present, is the attacker for kill attribution and death messages. The damage type bypasses the normal damage cooldown and shields and is tagged for no automatic knockback so the custom impact knockback is not applied twice.

Repeated collision effects are controlled by `ImpactContactTracker.java`, not by temporarily making the struck entity non-collidable. Once an entity receives an impact, its UUID is latched as an active contact. Continued AABB overlap or another clipped rail movement refreshes that contact. While the contact remains latched, subsequent collisions still participate in physical collision and residual-speed handling, but repeated damage and custom knockback are suppressed.

`minecartImpactHysteresisTicks` controls how long a separated contact remains latched before the same entity can receive a fresh impact. It defaults to 5 ticks and accepts 0–20. Contact state is refreshed at both the start and end of the minecart tick, and separated contacts expire once the time since their last contact exceeds the current hysteresis value. The tracker reads the gamerule when expiring contacts, so changing the rule affects existing contact state rather than only newly created trackers.

Do not restore the previous pass-through approach as part of a collision fix. The current model intentionally keeps minecarts solid and solves repeated impacts through contact latching and hysteresis. Automatic mob pickup remains limited to slow carts: carts at or below the 2 blocks/second interaction threshold may use vanilla pickup behavior, while faster carts use impact handling.

The other major subsystem is server-authored trajectory playback. During a server tick, `MinecartRailTraversalMixin` first finds tracking players that negotiated support for the trajectory payload. If nobody can receive it, trajectory construction is skipped. `ServerTrajectoryBuilder.java` records time-stamped rail-boundary positions, appends the authoritative endpoint, detects meaningful motion, and removes samples that are time-linear between their neighbors. `MinecartTrajectory.java` stores the resulting tick trajectory and samples both position and direction; tangent calculation falls back to final velocity and then an orientation hint when necessary.

Trajectory delivery is stateful per recipient. `TrajectoryStreamState.java` emits explicit `START`, `CONTINUE`, and `END` phases. Two stationary frames close a stream, after which the server stops sending idle updates until motion resumes. Preserve these stream boundaries: they let the client distinguish a deliberate idle gap from missing packets.

`MinecartTrajectoryPayload.java` defines the `msf:traj` payload and is intentionally compact. It sends the entity ID as a VarInt and the server tick as a VarLong, packs stream phase and optional-orientation state into flags, keeps trajectory endpoints and final velocity at double precision, and encodes interior timestamps and start-relative positions as floats. Orientation is omitted when final velocity already provides a usable direction. Treat changes to this codec or payload identifier as protocol changes.

On the client, `ClientTrajectoryManager.java` owns per-entity playback. Packets feed `TrajectoryPlayback.java`, which buffers consecutive server ticks and normally requires one tick of lookahead before starting or advancing a run. Explicit `START` and `END` phases provide resynchronization and clean terminal playback. `MinecartSpeedFeaturesClient.java` advances playback at `START_WORLD_TICK`, before vanilla snapshots entity interpolation state.

While trajectory playback is active, `MinecartClientTrajectoryMixin.java` disables vanilla minecart position interpolation. The client starts the tick at the authoritative trajectory start, lets vanilla perform its client tick, then applies the authoritative endpoint and final velocity. Rendering samples the same trajectory between those endpoints.

Keep the entire visible minecart stack on that sampled path. `TrajectoryRenderHooks.java` and the client mixins align the cart, passengers, camera, orientation, culling, shadows, and debug hitboxes. `MinecartEntityRendererMixin.java` supplies trajectory position and tangent samples to the minecart renderer. `EntityRendererMixin.java` handles entity and passenger render offsets and visibility. `EntityRenderDispatcherMixin.java` retains the offset for shadows and debug hitboxes. `CameraMixin.java` applies the same correction to a mounted camera.

Preserve the ownership boundaries when modifying this code. Vanilla owns rail behavior inside each `moveOnRail` call and physical collision resolution inside `Entity.move`. `EntityMovementMixin` only removes the minecart-specific tiny-movement cutoff after that resolution. The traversal layer owns rail-to-rail continuation and the one-tick time budget. The impact layer owns damage, knockback, residual velocity, and continuous-contact suppression. The server owns authoritative logical motion, while the client trajectory system owns smooth presentation of that motion.

Unit tests live under `src/test/java/ilostmy_fish/`. Current coverage is concentrated on `ImpactContactTracker`, rail geometry and movement budgeting, trajectory playback/lookahead behavior, and trajectory stream state. In particular, `RailMovementBudgetTest` covers clipped collision movement as well as multi-rail time-budget behavior, and `ImpactContactTrackerTest` covers zero, one-tick, and multi-tick hysteresis plus continuous contact refresh. Several deterministic helpers and protocol components currently have no dedicated unit test in this branch, and that is intentional. Too many tests ultimately make development cumbersome. If you ever want to add a test, do not do so autonomously. Figure out what the test would do and why you want to add it, then pitch it to me. Use `./gradlew.bat test` for the unit suite and `./gradlew.bat build` for a full build.
